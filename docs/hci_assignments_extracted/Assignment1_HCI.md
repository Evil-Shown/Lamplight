Problem statement and justification for selection

Library users currently face challenges in efficiently finding and reserving library resources. In particular, users may be unable to determine whether a required book is available before visiting the library and may also have difficulty finding an available reading-room seat.

The research conducted for this project investigated these issues among different library stakeholders and explored their preferences regarding book reservation, reading-room seat booking, availability information, notifications, cancellations, waiting lists, and other reservation-related features

User research plan and rationale for chosen technique

A questionnaire-based survey was used as the primary user research method.

The questionnaire consisted of 20 questions covering:

Participant role and library usage.

Existing book and reading-room problems.

Book reservation requirements.

Reading-room seat booking requirements.

Reservation reminders and confirmations.

Cancellation and waiting-list preferences.

Overall acceptance of the proposed system.

Suggestions for improvement.

A total of 119 responses were collected.

Data analysis (method used and findings)

f. Data Analysis (Method Used and Findings)

We used quantitative analysis (frequency counts and percentages) on the closed-question responses, and light thematic grouping on the open-ended suggestions (Q20), since responses there clustered naturally into a small number of recurring themes (seat enforcement, comfort, group study space, real-time crowding visibility, and Wi-Fi/connectivity).

Key Findings

Book discoverability is a widespread problem: 57.3% of respondents (67/119) have been unable to find a book they wanted, spread across all four stakeholder categories (Undergraduate 30/52, Postgraduate 18/34, Faculty 10/19, Library Staff 8/12).

Seat availability is a recurring but less universal problem: 31.6% of respondents (37/119) reported "Often" or "Always" being unable to find a seat, and a further 29.1% said "Sometimes" — meaning roughly 6 in 10 respondents face at least occasional seat scarcity.

Speed, not novelty, drives adoption: "Faster book reservation" was the single most selected feature that would drive regular use (50/119, 42.7%), ahead of "Real-time availability updates" (33/119) and "Easy seat booking" (22/119).

Preference for automated seat discovery over manual search: "System automatically recommends a seat" (33/119) and "Filter by floor or section" (30/119) were both preferred over manually browsing a "List of available seats" (28/119), suggesting users want the system to do the searching for them rather than presenting a raw list.

Confirmation channel preference is split, not dominated by one channel: Mobile app notification (34/119), SMS (32/119), and Email (32/119) were nearly evenly split, with a meaningful minority (19/119) wanting no confirmation at all — this argues for a configurable notification channel rather than a single hard-coded one.

Users want a graceful no-show policy, not an automatic penalty: "Ask for confirmation before cancelling" (33/119) and "Allow cancellation anytime" (31/119) were both preferred over immediately "Automatically releasing the seat" (29/119), suggesting the system should default to a grace/confirmation step rather than instant forfeiture.

Open-text suggestions reinforce enforcement and capacity themes: The most frequent free-text suggestion (9 of the ~62 respondents who left a comment) was that seats are booked but left empty and need better enforcement — directly corroborating the seat-scarcity finding above. Other recurring suggestions included more comfortable seating (7), longer booking hours (7), more group study rooms (6), and real-time crowd visibility (6).

User stories derived from research findings

As an Undergraduate Student, I want the system to automatically recommend an available seat, so that I don't have to walk through every floor to find one. (Traceable to Q9: "System automatically recommends a seat" was the top-ranked seat-finding method, 33/119.)

As a Postgraduate Student / Faculty Member, I want to see a book's current availability and exact shelf location before I travel to the library, so that I don't make a wasted trip. (Traceable to Q6: "Current availability" 39/119 and "Exact shelf location" 29/119 were the two most-requested pieces of pre-reservation information.)

As a Library Staff member, I want the system to ask for confirmation before cancelling a no-show seat reservation rather than cancelling it instantly, so that genuine short delays aren't unfairly penalised. (Traceable to Q14: "Ask for confirmation before cancelling", the top-ranked option at 33/119.)

As any registered user, I want to choose my own confirmation channel (SMS, email, or app notification), so that I receive reservation updates the way that suits me. (Traceable to Q13, where Mobile app notification, SMS, and Email were nearly evenly split at 34/32/32, with no single dominant preference.)

As an Undergraduate Student, I want to be added to a waiting list and notified when a seat frees up during busy periods, so that I don't have to keep re-checking manually. (Traceable to Q19: "Add me to a waiting list" was the top-ranked overflow option, selected in 23/119 single-choice responses and included in several combined responses.)
